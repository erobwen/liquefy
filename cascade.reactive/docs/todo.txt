Things to do:

- Rebuilding a large build() is slow, whatever changed. Every object a
  build() constructs is made anew, matched against the last run's
  (takeShape(), matchShape()) and merged into it (mergeInto()) - through
  the observables' proxies. A list of 300 cells built by a plain function
  (5,700 elements in one build()) takes 300-500 ms in Chromium to rebuild
  with nothing changed, or one cell moved. With each cell a component of
  its own, the list's build() constructs 300 components and the same
  change takes 50-80 ms: the cells' own builds don't run again. So for
  now: what's repeated many times should be a component. Ideas for the
  machinery itself, none tried:
  - takeShape(), matchShape() and mergeInto() read what the run
    constructed through the proxies (getHandlerObject is the largest
    single cost in the profiles, ownKeys and getOwnPropertyDescriptor
    follow). They could read the targets directly: these are the
    repeater's own, new objects, read without recording.
  - The shape is taken twice per run: once to match, once more at the
    end for the next run. The second could come from the first.

- Building and removing many components costs far more than the DOM work
  they do: replacing 300 cells (5,700 elements) takes 700-900 ms in
  Chromium - about 70 microseconds per element to take down and 90 to
  build - where the DOM's part is a few percent. About 3.5 partials per
  element. In the profile: time outside any function (presumably proxy traps and
  the like, a third), getHandlerObject (a sixth), removeAllSources,
  takeShape. Ideas, none tried:
  - Retract a subtree as a whole: dependencies inside it die with it,
    and only those on what's outside need unlinking; its partials are one
    stretch of the order chain, to cut out at once.
  - Fewer partials per element: see what a plain div with a text costs,
    and whether what reads nothing could share one.
